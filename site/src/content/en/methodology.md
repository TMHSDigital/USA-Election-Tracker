## What this site covers

The November 3, 2026 elections for the U.S. House (435 seats plus the District of Columbia delegate), the U.S. Senate (regular and special elections), and governor in 36 states. We show public data only. We do not forecast, rate races, average polls, or call winners.

## Candidate lists

Candidates come from official lists published by state election officials: certified candidate lists, official sample ballots, or official general election candidate listings. Every candidate row cites the official page it came from and the date we retrieved it. These lists are compiled by hand into files in our public repository and reviewed before publication.

Where we have not compiled an official list for a U.S. House or U.S. Senate race, we show candidates registered with the Federal Election Commission (FEC) for that race and say clearly that this is not the official ballot. FEC registration does not mean a person qualified for the ballot. Where neither is available, the page links to the state election office.

Party names appear exactly as the official source lists them, including abbreviations. Where a candidate appears on more than one party line, all lines are shown as the source lists them.

### Order

Candidates appear in ballot order when the official source gives one. Otherwise they appear alphabetically by last name. We never order by fundraising, party, or incumbency.

### Incumbents

A candidate is marked as an incumbent only if they currently hold that office: for the U.S. House, the seat with the same district number in the same state. After redistricting, a district with the same number may cover a different area, and a member running in a district with a different number is not marked as the incumbent of the new district.

## Campaign finance

Totals come from the FEC's bulk "all candidates" file for the 2025 to 2026 cycle. They cover each candidate's authorized committees through the coverage end date shown. Receipts, disbursements, and cash on hand are shown as the FEC reports them.

We link a candidate on an official list to an FEC record only when the match is unambiguous: same state, office, and district, the same last name, and a compatible first name, or a sitting member's FEC identifier from the congress-legislators project. When more than one record could match, or none does, we show "No FEC filing matched" rather than guess. Corrections to matches are recorded in a public overrides file.

Candidates for governor do not file with the FEC, so no federal finance data appears for them.

## District boundaries

Maps use U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line files for the districts in effect for the 2026 election (the 120th Congress files). The Census Bureau lists nine states with new maps for 2026: Alabama, California, Florida, Louisiana, North Carolina, Ohio, Tennessee, Texas, and Utah. For Missouri, the Census 2026 file contains a plan that was not permitted for 2026, so we use the plan in effect in 2024. Each state page names the law, ballot measure, or court order that set its boundaries.

Boundaries are simplified for display and for the lookup tool. The lookup warns you when a location is close to a boundary. Your state's official voter lookup is the authority.

## Past results

U.S. Senate results come from the MIT Election Data and Science Lab. U.S. House and governor results come from "Candidates in American General Elections" by Cha, Kuriwaki, and Snyder. Both are released under CC0. We show the five most recent general elections for each seat. House results are matched by district number, and district lines change after redistricting, so earlier results may describe a different area.

<h3 id="share-of-total">Share of total</h3>

A candidate's share of total is their votes divided by the total votes cast in that contest as reported by the source, multiplied by 100 and rounded to one decimal place. Totals in the source may include write-in and other votes not listed separately, so shares may not add to exactly 100.

## Demographics

Estimates come from the Census Bureau's American Community Survey 5-year data for 2020 to 2024, which is tabulated for the district lines used in 2024. For the nine states with new 2026 maps, those estimates describe different areas, so we show "Not available" and do not approximate.

## Election night

Election night pages link to each state's official results site. Any results we display are labeled unofficial, show the reporting status as the source states it, the source address, and when we fetched it. Our copies lag the source by several minutes or more. We never call or project races.

## Updates and failures

Data is refreshed by scheduled jobs. If a source fails or returns data that does not pass our checks, we keep showing the last good data with its original date, and the page shows an "out of date" notice.

## Corrections

Errors are fixed and recorded with a date on the Corrections page.
